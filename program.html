Keynote: Sketching the Boundaries: Rethinking Intelligent Analysis of System Provenance

At: Training Room 2

Speaker: Zhenkai Liang

Host: PC Chairs

▶More Details

Description: Response and prevention of cyber incidence requires analysis of information from multiple sources. Endpoint monitoring solutions continuously record system-level activities as audit logs, providing deep visibility into security incidents. Yet before designing increasingly sophisticated solutions, it is essential to understand what system provenance can reveal, how its information can be abstracted, and where intelligent analysis reaches its limits. In this talk, we discuss our recent line of work on abstracting behaviors and analyzing cyber threats. We explore how connections in programs and systems, such as finding the semantics from audit events or program structures, can uncover the semantics of components through their usage context of connections and identifies semantically similar behaviors. We discuss the boundaries imposed by information availability, abstraction, semantic ambiguity, and system complexity, as well as their implications for future AI-based system security solutions. Understanding these boundaries is a necessary foundation for determining what intelligent analysis should, or should not, attempt to achieve.

About Speaker: Zhenkai Liang is a professor in the Department of Computer Science, School of Computing, National University of Singapore. He is also a co-Lead Principal Investigator of National Security R&D Lab of Singapore. His research interests are in systems and security, including binary program analysis, system provenance analysis, cyber security experimentation, security in emerging platforms, such as Web, mobile, and Internet-of-things (IoT), and AI platforms. He also investigates security problems beyond the system boundary from the angle of economics, finance, and risk management. He has been publishing high-impact papers in top security and software engineering conferences and won ten best/distinguished paper awards and one test-of-time award, as well as seven Teaching Excellence Award from NUS and School of Computing. He is a current member of the Steering Committee of ACM CCS and has served as a member of the Steering Group of NDSS. He has served as technical committee members and editorial board members of top security conferences and journals. He received his Ph.D. degree in Computer Science from Stony Brook University in 2006 and B.S. degree in Computer Science and B.S. degree in Economics (CCER) from Peking University in 1999.

Keynote II: Security Agility: Challenges in Securing Products at Startup Velocities

At: Training Room 2

Speaker: Lok Yan

Host: PC Chairs

▶More Details

Description: Product security is the practice of securing products from design to destruction. This lifecycle challenge encompasses all of the security research areas from crypto, to systems, to networks, manufacturing, etc. Startup culture is indicated by accelerating agility. Innovation velocities are necessarily high where the direction changes as lessons are constantly learned while the magnitude only increases, rarely does it slow down. Combine the breadth and longevity of the problem with rapidly evolving landscapes and we have very exciting challenges. This talk is about those challenges, what I have found to work so far, and encouragement for those who want to drive research to solutions at startup speeds.

About Speaker: Lok has a diverse background that spans academia, government, and private industry. He is currently a product security engineer creating practical solutions to fast evolving product security challenges. Prior to joining Anduril he has served as program manager funding leading research in hardware and software security and driving them to usable solutions. He also served as Adjunct Professor for cybersecurity.
